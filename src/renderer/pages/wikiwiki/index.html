Wiki Wiki: the Quick Web

You found a hidden page. It belongs to the 2001 Horizon skin and honours one computer and one person from the early web.

The computer Power Mac G4 Cube

Is it a computer or a sculpture? Unveiled on July 19, 2000, the Power Mac G4 Cube was “an eight inch cube suspended in a stunning crystal-clear enclosure” [source 18]. Its cooling design “does not require a fan, so it runs in virtual silence” [source 18], which made it perfect for late-night browsing. The 450 MHz model cost $1,799 (US) and arrived in early August [source 18].

Most shoppers chose the bigger tower models instead, and on July 3, 2001, Apple announced it would suspend production of the Cube indefinitely [source 19].

But the art world kept it. New York’s Museum of Modern Art holds a G4 Cube, credited to Jonathan Ive and the Apple Industrial Design Group, in its design collection [source 20], and it was among the pieces chosen to greet visitors when MoMA’s design galleries reopened in 2004 [source 21]. It was a one-year wonder, frozen in see-through plastic and about as early-2000s glossy as a computer gets.

The person Ward Cunningham

Ward Cunningham made the web writable by everyone. On March 25, 1995, he started the WikiWikiWeb, “the first ever wiki site”, as an automated supplement to the Portland Pattern Repository [source 22]. He traced the wiki idea back to a HyperCard stack he wrote in the late 1980s [source 22].

The name comes from his first visit to Hawaiʻi, when an airline agent pointed him to the airport shuttle, the “Wiki Wiki Bus”, and explained that wiki wiki meant quick [source 23]. He chose it because “wiki wiki web” was more fun to say than “quick web” [source 23]. By the site’s tenth anniversary in 2005 it held about 30,690 pages [source 22].

In February 2001 he was one of the seventeen people who met at Snowbird, Utah, and wrote the Manifesto for Agile Software Development [source 24]. That January, a free encyclopedia went live with “wiki” in its name: Wikipedia [source 25]. Quick, quick indeed.
